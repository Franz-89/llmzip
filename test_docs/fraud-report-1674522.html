Fraud Vector #1 — Ticket Station Recovery Code Abuse

43 scan operations • ~120 individual recovery barcodes • ~8,155 tickets loaded

How Ticket Station recovery codes work: When a customer inserts paper tickets into a Ticket Station, the machine counts them and asks the customer to tap their card within 90 seconds. If no card is tapped, the machine prints a recovery barcode. This barcode can later be redeemed at the cashier onto any card.

Normal scenario: A customer without a card plays games, wins paper tickets, inserts them into the Ticket Station, but has no card to tap. They go to the cashier, buy a card, and the staff scans the recovery barcode onto the new card. This should happen at most once per customer — because from that point on, the customer has a card and can tap it directly at the Ticket Station.

Why ~120 Recovery Barcodes on One Card Is Impossible

Card #1674522 received ~120 individual recovery barcodes across 43 scan operations over 33 days. This is not a legitimate customer scenario:

  • A customer only needs ONE recovery barcode — the first time, before they have a card. After buying a card, they tap it directly at the Ticket Station. There is no reason for the same card to keep receiving recovery barcodes.
  • Multiple barcodes in a single operation — on 22/03, Kevin scanned 6 barcodes at once (13:28), then 4 more three minutes later (13:31), then 4 more that evening (19:18). This means someone had a collection of codes ready to scan.
  • Barcodes accumulated over different days — these codes were scanned across 22 different days. A customer would not keep losing their card, getting recovery codes, and then remembering to bring all of them to the same cashier.
The likely mechanism: When customers don't tap a card within 90 seconds, the Ticket Station prints a recovery barcode. These unclaimed barcodes are left behind or collected by staff. Kevin then scans these orphaned codes onto his own card at the cashier, accumulating tickets that belong to other customers (or to no one). Alternatively, staff may use the manual ticket add function at the cashier — which triggers the machine to produce a recovery code that Kevin then claims.

Conclusion & Recommendations

Card #1674522 • 09/04/2026

Verdict: CONFIRMED FRAUD

This is fraud. The evidence is conclusive and does not admit an innocent explanation.

Kevin (user 2785) systematically inflated the ticket balance on card #1674522 by harvesting ~120 Ticket Station recovery barcodes and scanning them onto his card across 43 operations over 33 days. A recovery barcode is a one-time fallback for a customer who inserts paper tickets into a Ticket Station but has no card to tap. A legitimate customer would need at most one recovery barcode — after that, they have a card and tap it directly. There is no scenario in which 120 recovery codes legitimately end up on the same card.

In addition to the barcode abuse, 15 card joins transferred 7,765 tickets from 11 source cards — including a card with zero game history (pure ticket aggregator) and a 3-level feeder chain. The scheme accelerated dramatically in March–April 2026, culminating in a Game Projector 4K (7,300 tickets) prize redemption on 07/04/2026.

The card still holds 14,992 tickets.

Why There Is No Innocent Explanation

  • ~120 recovery barcodes on one card is impossible for a customer. Recovery codes are generated when someone inserts paper tickets but doesn't tap a card within 90 seconds. Once a customer has a card, they tap it directly. Kevin's card already existed when all 120 codes were scanned onto it.
  • Multiple codes scanned at once proves collection. On 22/03/2026, Kevin scanned 17 barcodes in 5 operations within 6 hours. On 01/04, 8 barcodes at once. This is someone with a stash of codes, not a customer who forgot to tap.
  • Codes scanned across 22 different days over 33 days. No customer accumulates recovery codes day after day while already having a card.
  • Only 90 EUR of real money was ever loaded, yet the card has processed ~24,000+ tickets — ~67% from non-game sources.
  • A zero-play aggregator card (1862434) was used to funnel tickets from other cards through joins. This card never played a single game — it existed solely as a ticket collection point.

Key Findings

  • Primary fraud mechanism: Kevin scanned ~120 Ticket Station recovery barcodes onto card #1674522 in 43 operations (33 by Kevin himself). These are orphaned codes from customers who didn't tap, or codes generated via manual ticket adds at the cashier.
  • Secondary mechanism: 15 card joins from 11 source cards transferred 7,765 tickets. Kevin performed 8 of these joins directly.
  • Feeder card network: Card 1862434 (zero game history) served as a ticket aggregator with a 3-level chain. Teanny built the intermediate level; the extent of her awareness is unclear.
  • Prize extraction: 9,650 tickets already redeemed for physical prizes, including the Game Projector 4K (7,300 tickets) on 07/04/2026 — 2 days after the last card joins.
  • 14,992 tickets remain on the card, at risk of further prize redemption.
