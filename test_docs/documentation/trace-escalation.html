When a problem is beyond what your team can solve on its own, you can hand the whole trace — with its full context and history — to the SmartMonkey support team. This guide explains how that works, who is allowed to do it, and what happens next.

New to Trace? Start with Trace — Overview & how to use it. This guide assumes you already know what a trace is and how to work one.

What escalation is

Escalating a trace pushes it to SmartMonkey — the people behind the platform. Everything that makes the trace what it is travels with it: the entity it's attached to, the description, the whole timeline of what's been tried, the attachments. SmartMonkey staff can then work on it directly and report back, all on the same trace.

It is the bridge between "things the shop or the manager can handle" and "things that need the people who built the system" — a configuration change, an authorisation, a deeper investigation, a bug.

Escalation is not the first step. Try to resolve a trace within your team first. Escalate when you genuinely need SmartMonkey — a clear, specific ask — not as a way to pass along something that hasn't been looked at yet.

How it works, end to end

Escalation is a two-way link between your trace and SmartMonkey. Nothing leaves Trace — the trace stays yours; SmartMonkey simply joins in on it.

  1. You escalate the trace with a short note describing what you need. The trace and its history are sent to SmartMonkey, and an entry is added to the timeline so there's a record of when and why it was escalated.
  2. SmartMonkey picks it up. When a staff member takes the escalation, the trace shows Reviewing.
  3. SmartMonkey works and replies. Their updates come back onto the trace, so the conversation stays in one place.
  4. SmartMonkey finishes their part. The trace shows SM done. Their job is done — yours may still continue (you might need to apply something on your side, or simply close the trace).

"SM done" is not the same as "Resolved". SmartMonkey marking their part complete is independent of your trace's status. The trace stays open until you resolve it — because only you know whether everything is truly sorted on the shop side.

How to escalate a trace

  1. Open the trace.
  2. Find the SmartMonkey escalation strip and click Escalate to SmartMonkey.
  3. Write a short, specific note — the one thing you need (an authorisation code, a missing setting, "this game shows X, we already tried Y and Z").
  4. Send.

Make the trace tell the story first. The strength of an escalation is the context that travels with it. Before you send: is the right entity attached? Is what you've already tried written in the timeline? Are the useful photos there? A well-filled trace gets a fast, accurate answer; a one-line trace just gets questions back — which is slower for everyone.

Reading the status

Once escalated, the trace carries a badge that tells you, at a glance, where it stands with SmartMonkey:

  • Escalated — sent, waiting for SmartMonkey to pick it up.
  • Reviewing — a SmartMonkey staff member has taken it and is working on it.
  • SM done — SmartMonkey has finished their part.

If the latest reply on a trace came from SmartMonkey, the card flags it — so you can see at a glance which traces are waiting on your next move.

Re-escalating

You can escalate the same trace again if you need to — for example, if a new detail comes up. Each escalation is counted, so the history is clear.

If SmartMonkey already marked the trace done and you have a different question, prefer opening a new trace rather than re-escalating the old one. It keeps each trace's history clean and to the point — one problem, one thread.

Who can escalate — permissions

Escalation is controlled by a single permission (trace_escalate). The rule is simple:

  • SmartMonkey staff and system / API users have it automatically.
  • Your own team — managers and employees — do not have it by default. It is granted per user, so you decide exactly who on your side is allowed to escalate.

Because it's a normal permission, it can also be removed from an individual user at any time — even one who would otherwise have it — if you don't want them escalating.

Don't see the Escalate option? Then you don't currently have the permission. The button is simply hidden for users who can't use it. Ask your manager to grant trace_escalate if you need it.

Granting it (for managers)

To let someone on your team escalate, add the trace_escalate permission to their user, in the same place you manage that user's other permissions. To stop someone escalating, remove it. The change takes effect immediately — the Escalate option appears or disappears for them accordingly.

Escalation reaches outside your team. An escalation puts a request in front of SmartMonkey, so grant the permission deliberately — to the people who should be the point of contact with SmartMonkey, not to everyone by default.

In short

  • Escalate when you genuinely need SmartMonkey — after trying within the team.
  • Fill the trace first; the context is what makes the answer fast.
  • Watch the badge: Escalated → Reviewing → SM done.
  • SM done means SmartMonkey is finished — you still decide when the trace is Resolved.
  • Only users with trace_escalate can escalate; managers grant it per person.
