What a permission actually is, why we describe access as an area plus a level, and how to think about "what can this person do?" without getting lost in technical names.

The idea in one sentence

A permission answers a single question: “is this person allowed to do this here?” Everything below is just a tidy way of asking that question consistently across the whole application.

Area × Level

Access is described by two things together:

  • The area — a part of the application. Locations, Cash, Warehouse, Reporting, Users, Organizations, Customers, Trace, Marketing, Pricing, Devices, and so on. Each screen belongs to exactly one area.
  • The level — how much you can do in that area. There are two: View (look, but don't change) and Manage (create, edit, delete).

So a permission is always a pair — “Cash · View”, “Warehouse · Manage”, “Users · Manage”. That pairing is the whole model. Once you see access this way, every screen in the app has an obvious answer to “who gets in, and can they change anything?”

Manage always includes View. If someone can Manage an area, they can obviously see it too — you never grant both by hand. Grant Manage and View comes with it.

Why two levels and not just “on/off”

Most real roles need to see far more than they should change. An accountant needs to read every cash session and invoice but must never edit one. A shop manager runs their own location but only looks at the company-wide numbers. Splitting each area into View and Manage lets you hand out exactly that: read-only where it's information, full control where it's their job.

A few areas are single-level. Some things don't split into view/manage — either you do them or you don't (for example, escalating a Trace, or a technician's NFC tag assignment). For those, having the permission at all is the access.

Big areas contain smaller ones

Some areas are broad and made of parts. Locations is the clearest example: it's really a stack of tabs — Info, Opening hours, Games, Products, Floor, Stock, and more — each of which can be handed out on its own. Granting the whole Location area is a shortcut that hands out every tab at once; granting a single tab is how you give someone a narrow slice.

The same idea covers Reporting: there's an “all standard reports” permission, and there's one permission per individual report. Give the parent for everything, or a single report for someone who should only ever see that one.

You almost never build access from scratch. Every role starts from a sensible default bundle (see Permissions — The default starting point). Areas and levels are what you reach for when you want to adjust one person — add a report here, take away an area there.

What this replaces

The application used to lean on a blunt idea of “is this an admin?” — one big label that either opened a door or didn't. That made it impossible to say “this person handles reservations and customer look-ups, nothing else.” Area × Level fixes that: access is now made of small, named, combinable pieces, so a role can be exactly as wide or as narrow as the job really is.
