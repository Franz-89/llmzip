Analyse the physical cash your acceptors take in — how much, in which coins, on which games, and at what times.

Introduction

The Coin-in report is the analysis view of the coin log. Over any date range it shows the total the acceptors reported — everything logged in the period, whether or not it has been collected yet — and slices it so you can understand your cash business, not just measure it.

This is the "whole period" number. Unlike Cash in Box (only what's still in the machine) or Reconciliation (only what a collection has captured), Coin-in shows the full total for the dates you pick. That's why it is usually the largest of the coin figures.

Opening the report

Go to Analysis → Coin-in. Choose a date range, a location, and a period to compare with (the previous period, same period last year, and so on). Press Apply.

What it shows

Totals — the coin-in for the period, the number of coins, the average per coin and per machine, and how many coins were unpriced or invalid.

Coin mix — the breakdown by denomination: how much came in as each coin value. This is the shape of your cash intake.

By game — coin-in aggregated by game across all its machines. The clearest way to see which titles are your cash earners.

By location and top machines — the same total seen per shop and per individual machine.

The coin-flow heatmap

A day-of-week × hour grid showing when coins physically go in. Darker cells are busier. This reveals the rhythm of your floor — the evenings and weekends that carry the cash intake — at a glance.

The heatmap is coloured by coin count, not money. That keeps it meaningful even for machines whose coin values were only configured recently — the pattern of when people play is fully visible regardless. Switch it to euros with the toggle, and hover any cell for both figures. Turn on a comparison period to see now, before, or the difference between them.

The daily trend

Coin-in day by day across the range, with the comparison period overlaid, so you can see growth, dips, and the effect of events or promotions.

Two figures to keep in mind

Unpriced coins are logged but of unknown value (configured before their pin values were set). They appear in the coin counts but add nothing to the money, and are shown separately so a low total is explainable.

Invalid coins are impossible bursts from a faulty pulse line, already removed from the totals. A machine producing many of them likely has a wiring fault.

When to use it

  • Understanding your cash mix — which denominations and which games drive coin intake.
  • Spotting patterns — the heatmap and daily trend show when the floor earns, and whether it's growing.
  • Comparing periods — this week vs last, this month vs last year, with the difference laid out for you.

Coin-in is for insight, not collection. To decide what to physically collect right now, use Cash in Box. To check a count was honest, use Reconciliation. Coin-in is where you go to understand the business behind the coins.
