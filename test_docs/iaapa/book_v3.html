What it is

What SapiensCore actually is

SapiensCore is the operating system for an entertainment venue, built around one thing: the loyalty card.

The card is the spine. Through it, one system runs your POS (counter, tables, open tabs), your cashless payments — loyalty and bank cards on the same reader, at the counter and on every game — your bookings, your device monitoring, your day-to-day operations (cash collection flow, maintenance, staff accountability — every job tracked as a Trace), and your accounting. It runs on hardware we install in your venue, so it keeps working when the internet doesn't.

The loyalty card is the centre of gravity — it's what ties the customer to balance, history, tier, and VAT, and the platform is built for networks: multi-site operators running one system across every venue. You're never forced to buy the plastic from us — source the physical cards anywhere you like, or bring the ones you already use; the system that runs them is the part that's ours. If you don't run a cashless loyalty program at all, it still works — but you'd see loyalty woven through screens you'll never use, so honestly, it's not the best fit for you.

You see, and you act

Most monitoring tells you one thing: is the device on or off. Nowhere near enough — arcade hardware is decades old and rarely raises its hand to say it's broken; you learn by what stops happening. The PulseBox on every game sees every coin, payment, game start, and ticket, so a machine that normally starts twenty games an hour and suddenly starts zero shows up immediately — not when a customer complains.

Two minutes later it's back; the reader re-enables itself and the alert closes. Alerts that open and close themselves — signal, not twenty acknowledgements a day you stop reading.

And you don't just watch — you act, from the same screen or your phone: reboot a stuck device, start a game (a service test, or a free play for a customer whose last one didn't fire), block a terminal, push a price to every terminal in seconds, run pin-level diagnostics, even flash firmware remotely. Every command is logged: who, when, which device, from where.

You verify, you don't hope

Most operators run on trust — the collector, the cashier, the prize counter. Trust is fine until something goes wrong; then it gives you nothing. SapiensCore is built so everything important produces its own evidence, automatically.

  • Cash that reconciles clean — the coin counter is isolated, so the gap between counter and cash is always a real signal, never physics.
  • Counter photographed, OCR-verified — on collection; a typed number that disagrees alerts before the cash leaves the machine. Reading the counter and counting the cash are split across people, on purpose.
  • Cash tracked to the bank — collection → safe → transport → bank, each handoff logged; an alarm if it doesn't arrive in the window.
  • Patterns, not just records — 120 recovery codes redeemed onto one card isn't a customer, it's a staff-and-friend. Flagged the moment volume exceeds plausibility.
  • Join-chain analysis — balances funneled card-to-card-to-card, followed the whole way, no depth limit, in seconds.

None of this is about catching people. It clears your honest staff by default. Honour is wonderful — it just isn't a control system.

We go deeper · the chapter your accountant should read

Credit-VAT

A top-up isn't a sale. It's a deposit — the customer prepaying for a service they haven't received. Yet almost every system declares VAT the moment the card is loaded, so you pay tax on credit your customers may never spend.

With Credit-VAT, SapiensCore separates the top-up from the taxation. €20 loads €20 of credit; no VAT yet. VAT is calculated the moment that credit is consumed — on the exact amount, at the right rate for the right service (game play one rate, food another, a party room another). Bonus and courtesy balances are consumed first (the customer's paid credit lasts longer), regular credit next, Credit-VAT last (your liability stays at the legal minimum). Staff decide nothing; the customer sees one number; your books are simply correct.

Why does almost nobody do this? You have to understand the business from the inside, the implementation is genuinely hard, and it splits your reports in two — operational revenue today versus taxable revenue today. Most systems can barely produce one report correctly. We produce both from the same events.
