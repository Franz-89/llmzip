Intelligent Consumption Priority

When a customer plays a game, SapiensCore automatically decides the order in which balances are consumed. The logic is designed to maximize your business:

  • Gifts first. Balances that were given for free are consumed before anything the customer paid for. This protects the customer's purchased credit and ensures your promotional spend is used before real revenue is touched.
  • Regular credit next. The money the customer actually paid — where VAT was already charged at the moment of top-up. Think of all pre-SapiensCore era.
  • Credit-VAT as last resort. Because the moment Credit-VAT is consumed, you become VAT-liable for that exact amount. So the system uses it only when there is no other alternative. This minimizes your tax obligations to the absolute legal minimum.

SapiensCore manages all six balance types simultaneously on every card. A single transaction can consume multiple balance types at once — for example, 0.20 from bonus + 1.60 from credit in one play. All partial movements are calculated and applied automatically. Staff never make this decision. The system does.

Why this matters: This isn't just an accounting optimization — it's a business strategy. Customers see their purchased credit lasting longer because free balances are used first. Credit-VAT is protected until absolutely necessary, keeping your tax obligations correct and minimized. And because partial consumption across multiple balance types is automatic, there's no rounding, no approximation, no manual intervention — every cent is tracked to the correct balance, on every single transaction.

Credit & VAT — The Revenue You Don’t See

This might be the most important page in this document.

The problem

When a customer tops up their loyalty card with 10 EUR, most systems charge VAT immediately — on the full amount, at the point of sale.

That is wrong.

A top-up is not a sale. It's credit. The customer is prepaying for future use. VAT should only be calculated — and declared to tax authorities — when that credit is actually spent. Not when it's loaded.

This distinction sounds academic. It's not. It's money. Your money, to be precise — sitting in the tax authority's pocket instead of yours.

Here's a simple test you can do right now.

Open the software you're currently using. Ask: how much credit is sitting on customer cards, unused?

Take that number. Calculate the VAT on it.

That's what you've already paid to the tax authorities — and you shouldn't have.

That money is gone. It was declared as revenue, taxed, and paid. But the customer never used it. They might never use it. And you can't get it back.

Why no one else does this

Three reasons.

First, you need to deeply understand how these businesses actually operate. Not from a marketing slide — from the inside. How credit flows, how bonus is given, how partial plays work, how a customer can use credit for games today and food tomorrow. Most software companies have never worked at this level.

Second, it's genuinely hard to implement. Consider what actually happens:

  • A customer tops up 10 EUR. They receive 10 EUR credit + 2 free bonus.
  • They play a game that costs 1.80 EUR. The system uses 1.80 from bonus first (consumption priority). No credit is consumed — so no VAT is due on this play.
  • Next game: 1.80 EUR again. Bonus is only 0.20 left, so 0.20 from bonus + 1.60 from credit. VAT is calculated on exactly 1.60 — not 1.80.
  • Later, the customer uses credit to pay for a birthday party. Different VAT rate. The system handles it.
  • The customer also buys food with their credit. Different VAT rate again. Handled.
  • Some credit expires after 18 months. That expired credit needs to be accounted for too.

Every single transaction needs to know: how much credit was consumed, at what VAT rate, and for what type of service. The system tracks this automatically, in real time, for every play, every purchase, every partial balance consumption.

Third, it breaks your reporting. When a top-up is no longer a sale for accounting purposes, your sales reports and your accounting reports will show different numbers — and they should. A 10 EUR top-up is revenue from an operational perspective, but it's not a taxable sale until the credit is used. So now you need two views of the same data: one for operations (how much money came in today) and one for accounting (how much VAT-liable revenue was generated today). Most systems can barely produce one correct report. SapiensCore produces both, automatically, from the same underlying data — because it tracks the distinction at the transaction level.

VAT-Postponed Credit (Credit-VAT)

When enabled, SapiensCore separates the top-up moment from the taxation moment. Credit loaded onto a card is stored as VAT-postponed — no VAT is calculated, no VAT is declared, no VAT is paid to the tax authority. VAT is only calculated at the exact moment the credit is consumed — on the exact amount consumed — at the correct VAT rate for the specific product or service.

You have to do literally nothing.

The system handles the consumption priority, the partial amounts, the different VAT rates, the bonus separation, the expiration accounting, and the fiscal declarations. It generates the correct data automatically: because you also need to account for the credit-VAT in your books. Your accounting team sees clean, correct numbers. Your tax declarations are compliant.

Top-up Intelligence

This is one of those features that sounds small but changes everything at the counter.

Imagine a promotion: "Top up 20 EUR and get extra bonus." A customer walks up and asks for 10 EUR. The employee adds 10 EUR. Then, before paying, the customer changes their mind: "Actually, let's go for the 20 EUR promo."

In most systems, the employee has to remove the 10 EUR, find the specific 20 EUR promotional package, and add that instead. In Sapiens: it doesn't matter. The employee just adds another 10 EUR. SapiensCore automatically finds the best optimization for that customer — combining the two top-ups into whatever package or promotion gives the best result.

But it goes further. If for that specific customer, based on their tier, their rules, their configuration, it's actually better to not apply the 20 EUR promo but something else entirely — SapiensCore will do it. No one needs to notice. No one needs to decide.

Why this matters more than you think. Sometimes a promo gives just a couple of extra bonus. If it's not applied correctly, nobody notices — until the customer finishes their bonus and comes back complaining it's missing. What do you do then? Manually add bonus? That opens a security hole. The best way to fix a problem is to not have the problem in the first place. That's also at the core of our philosophy.

Time Balance — Smarter Than "Unlimited Play"

Time-based play sounds simple: buy 30 minutes, play as much as you want. Simple to explain to a customer. Surprisingly difficult to implement without creating a complaint factory.

Activation: You choose when time starts — at the moment of purchase, or on first play. If a customer buys time at 2 PM but doesn't start playing until 4 PM, should they lose 2 hours? You decide.

Scope: Time can be valid at specific locations, on specific games, or globally. Fully configurable.

Fair use: You can set a minimum interval between plays (e.g., 1 game every 30 seconds) to prevent rapid exploitation.

The credit protection problem: Imagine a customer is playing with time. They try a game that isn't covered by their time package. Most systems would silently charge their credit balance. The customer thinks it's covered by time — it's not. They find out later, and they're upset. SapiensCore blocks the transaction entirely. The game won't start. Because allowing it would mislead the customer into thinking time was paying for it.

What happens when time expires? Many systems do this: time runs out, customer has credit on the card, the next game silently charges credit. The customer keeps playing, doesn't realize time ended, and later complains they didn't authorize credit use.

SapiensCore does something different. The first transaction after time expires is always refused. Always. Even if the customer has credit. A clear message is shown: "Your time has ended. From now on, normal credit will be used." Only after this explicit notification will subsequent games use credit.

One refused game. Zero complaints. Zero disputes. Zero manual corrections.

Most systems give you a discount button. Anyone at the counter can tap it, apply 10%, and move on. No rules, no trace, no accountability. The principle: if a discount can be automated via a membership rule, it should be. Discount buttons exist for the exceptions. The fewer times they're pressed, the better your system is configured.

Multi-Card Operations in One Transaction

At the counter, reality is messy. A family walks up with three cards. One needs a top-up, one needs to be replaced, and they want to buy a new card for a friend. In most systems, that's three separate transactions, three separate payment flows, three chances for something to go wrong.

In Sapiens, it's one operation.

  • Join multiple cards at once: Select several cards and merge them all into one in a single operation. All balances transfer. Full audit trail.
  • Top up multiple cards at once: Add the same package to every card (e.g., 10 EUR each), or different packages on different cards — all in one transaction, one payment.
  • Sell new cards in the same operation: Activate one or more new cards, top it up, and include it in the same batch with existing cards being topped up.
  • Add extra products: Food, drinks, merchandise — add them to the same transaction alongside card top-ups. One receipt. One payment.

This isn't a convenience feature. It's a speed multiplier at the counter. Fewer transactions means shorter queues, faster service, and fewer errors.

Relevant History

Every loyalty card in SapiensCore has a complete, reconstructable history. Not just "what happened" but "why it happened, who did it, and what the state was before and after."

But the key word is Relevant. Here's what it means:

When Card A is joined into Card B (with A as the primary), the history of Card B doesn't disappear. It becomes part of Card A's history — specifically, everything that happened on Card B from its last activation or sale until the moment it was joined. Because that history is relevant to the customer, not to the physical card.

The customer doesn't care that the plastic changed. They care about their balance, their plays, their transactions. Relevant History follows the customer's journey, not the card's serial number. When cards are joined, you don't lose track of anything.

This is time-sensitive — you can reconstruct the exact state at any point in time. And it supports scenarios that would break most systems:

  • Card A is joined into Card B. Then the customer returns Card B, so you sell it again to someone else. The new customer tops it up, plays, and later joins it into Card C. SapiensCore traces every step — the original join, the return, the re-sale, the new top-ups, and the final merge — with the correct balances at every stage.
  • A customer disputes their balance. You reconstruct every credit movement, every play, every join, every top-up — and show them exactly where every cent went, at what time, by whom.
  • A staff member is suspected of fraud. You pull every operation they performed across every card, with timestamps, amounts, and the card's state before and after each action.
Relevant History isn't a log viewer. It's the customer's complete financial story — across any number of card merges, returns, re-sales, and transfers. It doesn't matter how many physical cards were involved. It answers: "What is this customer's full history?" — with evidence, not guesswork.

Game Collection & Cash Tracking

From the coin box to the bank — every euro is tracked, verified, and accounted for.

Cash collection from arcade machines is one of the biggest theft vectors in entertainment venues. SapiensCore provides a rigorous, multi-step workflow that separates duties, cross-verifies amounts, and generates legally compliant receipts — with full audit trails at every stage.

How It Works: Counter vs. Cash Separation

The system intentionally separates two distinct moments in the collection process:

StepWhoWhat Happens
1. Counter Reading Collection staff Reads the mechanical counter on the arcade machine. The counter increments with every coin inserted. This gives the expected revenue.
2. Cash Counting Cash handling staff Opens the coin box and physically counts the cash. This gives the actual revenue. This happens at a different time, often by a different person.
3. Deviation Check System (automatic) Calculates the difference between expected (counter) and actual (cash). Deviations above threshold are flagged.
4. Accounting Approval Accounting team Reviews flagged collections, approves or rejects with comments, sets the final accounting value.
5. Fiscal & ERP System (automatic) Approved collections are sent to Odoo (ERP) and, if required by local law and you wish to have this automatism, to the tax authority as well. Receipts are generated.
Why separate counter and cash? Because if the same person reads the counter and counts the cash, they can manipulate both numbers to match. By requiring two independent confirmations — often by different staff members — the system creates a natural check. If someone takes cash from the coin box, the counter reading will reveal the discrepancy.

When a game also accepts Nayax card payments, the system automatically subtracts card transactions from the expected cash amount. The formula is:

Expected Cash = (Counter Diff − Nayax Counter Diff) × Price Coefficient

This prevents false discrepancies on machines with mixed payment methods. Note: if you use our PulseBox, this step is not needed at all — because Nayax transactions don't make the physical counter spin in the first place. One more reason PulseBox changes the game.

  • Multiple codes in one operation: Staff scanning a batch of collected codes at once reveals systematic harvesting
  • Cross-staff tracking: Which staff member scanned each barcode, enabling identification of the person inflating tickets
